Policy-Based Trajectory Clustering

NeurIPS 2025 Submission by Xinqi Wang et al.

🌌 Overview

We propose a novel task in offline reinforcement learning: clustering trajectories by the policies that generated them.

  • PG-Kmeans: EM-style clustering with policy networks.
  • CAAE: VQ-VAE-inspired latent space clustering.

💡 Why Policy Clustering Matters

In offline reinforcement learning (RL), trajectory data is typically collected from a mix of unknown or partially known policies — agents with different levels of skill, intent, or exploration strategy.

  • Suboptimal Policy Aggregation: Mixing trajectories from distinct policies can confuse learning algorithms, leading to unstable updates or policy collapse.
  • Lack of Interpretability: Without understanding who did what, it’s hard to debug or make sense of policy decisions from data alone.

Our work addresses this by clustering trajectories by their generating policies. This idea allows us to:

  • Group behaviorally similar agents together, reducing noise and improving learning efficiency.
  • Enable semi-supervised labeling or fine-tuning within clusters.
  • Discover hidden behavior modes (explorers, experts, subpolicies).
  • Unify clustering, imitation learning, and generative modeling in one framework.

In short, policy clustering transforms the passive dataset into an active tool — one that structures and reveals the latent strategies hidden within RL behavior.

🔹 PG-Kmeans

Iteratively trains behavior cloning policies and clusters based on action likelihoods. Guarantees finite-step convergence.

🔹 CAAE

Learns latent representations attracted to Gaussian centroids. Efficient and robust under stochastic environments.

📊 Results

Our methods outperform conventional clustering (DEC, VAE+KMeans) and SORL across D4RL and custom GridWorld tasks.
